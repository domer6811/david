Where I help

The situations teams usually bring me in for, what I do about them, and where I've done it before.

Your SaaS product is outgrowing its architecture

When: new customers mean custom code, performance drops as tenants are added, or one big customer's workload slows everyone else down.

What I do: design multi-tenant data models, isolate tenant workloads, and separate reporting from transactional paths so growth doesn't mean a rewrite.

Proof: led architecture of a multi-tenant SaaS QA auditing platform used by thousands of people, with a shared reporting layer that served every tenant without hurting transactional performance.

Integrations fail, get throttled, or have to be babysat

When: third-party APIs return 429/503 errors, syncs fail overnight, or one engineer is the only person who understands the middleware.

What I do: redesign integration layers around caching, messaging, retries, and observability.

Proof: built a distributed caching framework that cut SharePoint Online API calls from 30+ per document to 1–2, eliminating throttling errors and greatly increasing throughput.

Your systems don't talk to each other

When: after an acquisition, an ERP rollout, or years of point-to-point connections that nobody wants to touch.

What I do: design event-driven and message-based integration architecture, plus migration paths off legacy platforms.

Proof: built 400+ system interfaces across healthcare, government, and commercial clients, led legacy-to-SAP integration at Fluor, and worked on NASA's IFMP integration architecture.

The database is the bottleneck

When: slow queries, reports that time out, or rising cloud database costs.

What I do: set up SQL performance standards, indexing strategy, and data-access patterns, then teach them to the team.

Proof: created SQL performance standards and tuning practices across large Azure SQL and SQL Server systems.

You need a technical co-pilot to get from idea to product

When: a founder or business owner has a validated idea but no senior architect to make the early decisions that are expensive to undo.

What I do: architecture, MVP build, cloud setup, and deployment pipelines, sized to the stage you're at.

Proof: designed and built the Portmason™ platform and launched its first application, ReviewNudge™, end to end.

You want AI to make your team faster, not just add noise

When: the team is experimenting with GenAI but hasn't changed how work actually gets done.

What I do: fit AI-assisted development and automation into real workflows, such as data processing, reporting, and operational tasks.

Proof: use AI-assisted workflows daily in my own platform work to automate data processing and reduce manual work.
